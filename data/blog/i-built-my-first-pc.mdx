I have built my first desktop PC recently, and I wanted to document the experience and share some insights here, perhaps helpful for some of you out there whose considering building their own.

## Let's start off with the Why(s)?

Well, I think I'm in a situation that demands more computing power. Currently, I'm using Asus Laptop with an 8th Intel Processor, 256 SSD, 1 TB HDD & 12 GB RAM DDR4. This laptop served me well during my university (doing programming, CAD software, game development etc.)

I choose to build a PC and not a lapyop because I wanted to have more control over the components and their quality. Plus, building a PC can be more cost-effective for the performance you get. Also, just for the fun of building it!

### The CPU

I think deciding for CPU taking most of the time because you know, Intel or AMD? AM4 or AM5? high speed or more cores? That kind of thoughts. This time I think I want to go with **AMD** CPUs due to cheaper price for the amount of cores you got. Again, I made my mind to future-proof my build, so I choose **AM5 platform** for this build and went with Ryzen 5 7600.

[Ryzen 5 7600](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/desktops/ryzen/7000-series/amd-ryzen-5-7600.html) has 6 cores and 12 threads (wow!). It has a base clock speed of 3.8 GHz and a boost clock speed of up to 5.1 GHz. It may not super powerful or whatever but the specs blown me away for the price that I can afford.

### The Motherboard

The backbone of the PC. AM5 peripherals tends to have higher prices compared to AM4 counterparts. I went for budget friendly motherboard with decent features; [ASRock A620M-HDV/M.2](https://www.asrock.com/MB/AMD/A620M-HDVM.2/index.asp). I figure I won't be doing any overclocking or whatsoever, so this motherboard is sufficient for my needs.

Some features of this motherboard includes:

- **AMD Socket AM5** - Supports Ryzen™ 9000, 8000 and 7000 Series Processors
- **DDR5 Memory** - 2 DIMM slots supporting Dual Channel, up to 6400+ (OC)
- **PCIe 4.0 x16** - For dedicated graphics card
- **NVMe Storage** - 1 Hyper M.2 slot (PCIe Gen4x4) for high-speed SSD

It covers all the essentials I need without the premium features I won't use.

## Problems & Troubleshooting

I had one issue about RAM. Sometimes the PC would freeze for a few seconds, then crash (by showing BSOD or just black screen). The error was `FAULTY_HARDWARE_CORRUPTED_PAGE` or something along those lines. After looking online, I figure that the issue may comes from the RAM. 

<figure className="mx-auto w-full max-w-md">
  <img className="w-full h-auto" src="https://res.cloudinary.com/iqfareez-cloud/image/upload/v1787560894/website/PC%20Build/buildpc_issue_page_fault_qbh15r.jpg" alt="RAM seated" />
  <figcaption className="text-center">Blue screen of death. The error message may varies but all were related to memory errors</figcaption>
</figure>

I had configured my RAM to use its EXPO profile, which set the speed to **6000 MT/s** (the [advertised speed](#the-memory)). Running RAM check doesn't show any errors. I thought perhaps I got faulty RAM but later I found out this is common problem with AM5 platforms which sometimes the memory controller/Motherboard (idk, really) couldn't handle the high speed of the RAM. I also make sure by Motherboard were running the latest BIOS version available.

I fixed this by lowering the speed to 5400 MT/s. The system no longer crashes, I'm happy with it. I think the speed & performance wouldn't be that significant after all. [At later time](my-pc-finally-optimized), I make it higher to 5600 MT/s and the system still stable.


[^1]: AM4 and AM5 are AMD's CPU socket types. AM4 is the older, more established platform (released 2016) with a wide range of compatible CPUs and motherboards at lower prices. AM5 is the newer platform (released 2022) supporting the latest Ryzen 7000+ series processors with features like DDR5 RAM and PCIe 5.0, but at higher prices.
